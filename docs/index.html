what p_1 is

p_1 is a small language model raised from nothing — no PyTorch, no libraries, no pretrained anything: an autograd engine, a byte-level BPE tokenizer, a transformer and AdamW, all written by hand and taught to each other in JS + Python twins. It is trained on a tiny health Q&A corpus and served whole in your browser — you are talking to the weights right now, not to an API.

The experiment: does grounding (answering only from retrieved corpus sentences), verification (claim-by-claim checking against the evidence) and abstention (refusing what the corpus cannot settle) reduce hallucination — measured on a model that hallucinates by construction? A myth-contamination A/B (same model trained with and without folk-belief traps in the corpus) probes where the nonsense comes from.

every part handmade

  • engine — tensor.js: reverse-mode autograd, broadcasting, fused crossEntropy, iterative backward.
  • tokenizer — tokenizer.js: byte-level BPE, 4,096 vocab, GPT-2-style rank merging.
  • transformer — model.js: pre-LN decoder with weight-tied head, identical to the Python twin.
  • AdamW — train.js: decoupled weight decay, warmup, global-norm clipping.
  • retrieval — retrieve.js: inverted index, BM25 + tf-idf cosine over 4,278 chunks.
  • verifier — verify.js: claim splitting, content-F1 verdicts, myth comparator, refusal detection.
  • weights bridge — weights.js: parses the Colab-exported web_model.bin into the browser model.
self-tests: tensor · tokenizer · model · JS↔NumPy parity · retrieval · train demo

honest limitations

This is a toy model that hallucinates on purpose — that IS the experiment, and the lab shows its failures as plainly as its successes. RAW mode invents folk remedies and wrong numbers; even GROUNDED mode is only as good as a 4,278-pair hobby corpus and a sentence-overlap verifier (no semantic understanding, no medical knowledge). Nothing here is medical advice: do not ask the baby for a diagnosis, ask a doctor.
